# Keiyian ERP — Presenter Notes for the Stakeholder Proposal

**Presented by:** Keiyian ERP team  
**Audience:** Keiyian Farmers Cooperative Society stakeholders  
**Stakeholder and contract discussion | Tuesday, 13 October 2026**  
**Presenter:** [Name / role]  
**Proposed duration:** 15–20 minutes, followed by discussion

## Purpose

We are presenting Keiyian ERP as a configurable operational platform for Keiyian Farmers Cooperative Society. Our aim is to show how the current platform can address the stakeholders' priorities, agree a suitable first delivery phase, and secure a mandate to complete discovery and prepare a costed implementation proposal and contract.

## Before the meeting

- Confirm the presenter, attendees, decision-makers, meeting venue and available demo environment.
- Prepare a short live walkthrough using a safe demo account and sample data. Do not use real patient or member information.
- Confirm the commercial position, implementation capacity, support model and any commitments that can be offered. Do not invent prices, guarantees, references or go-live dates.
- Ask the stakeholders to identify their top three operational pain points and the person authorized to approve scope and budget.

## Suggested opening

"Good morning, and thank you for meeting with us. We are the Keiyian ERP team, and today we will present how our platform can support Keiyian Farmers Cooperative Society's operational priorities. We will show selected workflows, explain a practical phased delivery approach, and seek your agreement on the priorities and next steps needed to shape a clear proposal and contract."

## Presentation flow and speaker cues

### 1. Start with their priorities — 2 minutes

**Say:** “A cooperative’s work crosses departments. Member and farmer services, production, procurement, stock, finance, people and management reporting all affect one another. Our goal is to help your teams work from connected processes and more consistent information.”

**Ask:** “Which three processes create the most delay, rework or difficulty getting a reliable picture today?”

**Cue:** Listen first. Repeat their priorities in their language and use them to choose the demo path. Do not assume the healthcare module is the main buying priority; confirm whether it is in scope.

### 2. Explain the opportunity — 2 minutes

**Say:** “Our Keiyian ERP platform brings a broad operational foundation into one application. It includes areas for farmers, livestock, animal feeds, dairy, agriculture, procurement, inventory, finance, HR, rentals, sales, reporting and workflows. These capabilities give us a starting point to configure connected processes and make routine work easier to follow.”

**Bridge:** “The value comes from fitting these capabilities to your policies, roles and day-to-day processes. We propose prioritizing the areas that matter most to you and phasing delivery around them.”

### 3. Demonstrate the workflow — 5–7 minutes

Choose only two or three paths that match what stakeholders said. Show the end-to-end work and the handoff between roles; avoid a screen-by-screen tour.

Possible path: **procurement → goods received → inventory → finance**.  
Possible path: **farmer/member record → dairy or agriculture activity → reporting**.  
Possible path: **request → review/approval workflow → decision visibility**.

**For each path:**

1. State the real-world task and the role responsible.
2. Show the starting record and the action taken.
3. Show the next team’s handoff and what they can see.
4. Explain what information management could use, subject to the agreed reporting scope.
5. Ask: “Would this match your process? What approval or exception would we need to account for?”

**Demo discipline:** If a screen, integration, report or permission is not available in the demo, say so plainly and capture it as a discovery or delivery item. Do not present a mock-up as a working capability.

### 4. Address healthcare scope accurately — 2 minutes, if relevant

**Say:** “The workspace contains an HMIS area for patient, visit, laboratory, prescription and billing workflows. The project records show that parts of the clinical flow have been hardened and regression-tested, but the complete hospital operating model is not yet established. Important areas such as admissions and discharge, insurance and claims, comprehensive audit, and deeper clinical controls remain planned work. We recommend treating HMIS as a separately scoped, staged rollout with clinical and operational sign-off before live use.”

**If asked whether it is production-ready:** “We should not describe the full HMIS as ready for broad production deployment. The documented recommendation is controlled delivery and further review of the remaining operational phases. We can define the required controls and acceptance gates with your clinical and finance leads.”

### 5. Propose a controlled delivery approach — 3 minutes

**Say:** “We propose beginning with discovery and a jointly approved scope, then delivering in small phases. Each phase should have named business owners, demonstrations, agreed acceptance criteria, training and a decision to proceed.”

**Suggested phases:**

1. **Discovery and fit:** confirm processes, roles, data, priorities, integrations, hosting, security, reporting and constraints.
2. **Foundation and priority workflows:** configure the highest-value agreed operational paths; prepare data and train pilot users.
3. **Pilot and acceptance:** test realistic scenarios, reconcile outputs, resolve findings and obtain business-owner sign-off.
4. **Rollout and support:** expand by agreed groups or modules, monitor adoption, and operate a documented support and improvement cycle.

**Scope control:** “We will convert the discovery findings into deliverables, exclusions, dependencies, milestones, responsibilities and commercial terms before committing to dates or price.”

### 6. Agree outcomes and measures — 2 minutes

**Say:** “We want success to be visible in your operation. At discovery, let us establish a baseline and agree targets for the first phase.”

Potential measures to select together:

- Time to complete the priority workflow.
- Number of records needing correction or duplicate handling.
- Time to produce an agreed management report.
- On-time completion of approvals and handoffs.
- Reconciliation differences between operational and finance records.
- Pilot-user completion and training readiness.

Do not promise numerical improvement until a baseline and target are jointly agreed.

### 7. Close with a specific decision — 2 minutes

**Say:** “If the direction fits your priorities, today we would like to agree the first discovery workshop, the business owners who should attend, the top workflows to scope, and the person who will coordinate the commercial review. From there we can return with a defined proposal and acceptance plan.”

**Ask for agreement on:**

- The first business areas and workflows to prioritize.
- A sponsor and departmental process owners.
- A date and participants for the discovery workshop.
- The information required for a costed proposal (users, sites, data, integrations, hosting and support expectations).
- The procurement and contract review process.

## Likely stakeholder questions

**“What does the system cover?”**  
“The current workspace spans core cooperative and business operations including farmers, agriculture, dairy, livestock, procurement, inventory, finance, HR, rentals, sales, reports and workflows. We will confirm which are in scope and fit them to your processes.”

**“How quickly can you deliver?”**  
“We can give a credible schedule after discovery confirms the modules, integrations, data readiness, decision turnaround and rollout approach. We would rather commit to a schedule supported by an agreed scope.”

**“What will it cost?”**  
“We should price the agreed scope, implementation, hosting, training, support and any integrations transparently. The next step is to gather those requirements and return with a costed proposal.”

**“Can we see evidence of quality?”**  
“Project delivery notes record 15 passing server regression tests and a successful client production build for the HMIS hardening work. Those are useful engineering checks; they do not replace acceptance testing against your business processes or prove every module is production-ready.”

**“Can we start with everything?”**  
“We can plan a broader roadmap, but a prioritized pilot gives your teams a way to validate fit, data and adoption before expanding. We can phase the contract around agreed outcomes.”

**“Can you guarantee savings or eliminate errors?”**  
“We can agree target measures and track them against a baseline. The result depends on scope, adoption, data quality and process decisions, so we should not guarantee an outcome before establishing those conditions.”

## Presenter reminders

- Lead with stakeholder pain points and outcomes, not a feature list.
- Keep the tone confident, collaborative and specific.
- Use only confirmed evidence; label proposals and future scope as proposals.
- Never promise a price, delivery date, integration, certification, uptime level or clinical readiness that has not been approved.
- Capture questions, owners and follow-up dates in a decision log.
- End by restating the agreed next step, owner and date.

## Internal evidence and limits

These notes are based on the Keiyian ERP workspace and its internal HMIS delivery, improvement and gap-analysis documents. The workspace exposes the listed operational modules. The HMIS hardening document records 15 passing server tests and a successful client build, while also recommending staged clinical rollout and further work on operational phases. Those records are project evidence, not independent certification or proof of customer outcomes. Commercial terms, integrations, hosting, security commitments, service levels and implementation dates have not been supplied and must be agreed before contract signature.
